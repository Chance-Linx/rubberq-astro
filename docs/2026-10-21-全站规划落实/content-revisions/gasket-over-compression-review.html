When a gasket leaks, tightening the assembly further should follow a review of the closed gap, gasket geometry and joint requirements. Record what was assembled and what changed before deciding whether the problem calls for a different material, a dimensional change or a revised installation procedure.

A flattened gasket is an observation to investigate. It does not, by itself, establish whether the original compression was excessive, the material changed during service or the joint moved under operating conditions.

Identify what controls the closed gap

Review the gasket drawing together with the mating parts. Identify whether stops, spacers, groove geometry or another feature limits closure. Record the gasket thickness and the dimensions of the closed assembly using an agreed measurement method.

If fasteners are involved, include the specified assembly sequence, lubricant and tightening method. Ask how joint closure is checked. Treat a fastening instruction and a measured gasket gap as different records; the review needs the information relevant to the actual joint.

Separate geometry, material and service observations

QuestionEvidence to collect
Was the intended gap achieved?Gasket and mating-part dimensions, tolerances, stops and assembly measurements
Where is the damage?Photographs showing edges, holes, corners, contact faces and orientation
Did the condition change in service?Operating time, fluid, temperature and pressure history, including cleaning
Was the same material installed?Compound identification, lot, drawing revision and available inspection records
Was the joint disturbed?Installation, maintenance, disassembly and any retightening history

Keep an unused gasket from the same lot where available. Record when and how the used part was removed and measured. Preserve both the observed condition and the original part identification so dimensional and material comparisons have a clear basis.

Do not transfer an O-ring rule to every gasket

The Parker O-Ring Handbook treats squeeze as a seal-design variable and distinguishes application arrangements. Its O-ring guidance should be read within that scope. A molded profile, flat gasket and O-ring can require different geometric reviews; agree the requirement for the actual component.

For a custom profile, show the section before and after assembly and identify the features intended to make contact. Ask which dimensions need control and how samples will be measured. Material hardness alone is not a complete drawing or installation specification.

Plan a comparison that answers the failure question

Write down the proposed change and the result it is intended to improve. If a new thickness, material or assembly setting is being considered, identify the comparison samples and the assembly conditions to keep consistent. Agree the leakage or functional test, operating exposure and inspection points.

A material compression-set result and an assembly leakage result describe different evaluations. Keep both with their test conditions rather than using one as a substitute for the other. If retained sealing force is important, include that requirement explicitly in the engineering discussion.
