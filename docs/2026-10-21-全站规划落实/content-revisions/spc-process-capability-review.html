When evaluating a rubber-part supplier's process capability, ask which characteristic was measured, which specification limits applied and how the data were collected. A Cpk value is useful only with that context. Agree the evidence required for your part and production conditions before using an index to support approval.

NIST's process-capability guidance connects capability analysis with a stable process, specification limits and the distribution of measured results. A reported index describes the characteristic and data evaluated; it does not establish that every property of every part will conform.

Start with the drawing characteristic

Identify the part number, drawing revision and feature. State the units, specification limits and measurement conditions. For rubber components, record whether dimensions were measured after molding, after any post-cure or after the agreed conditioning period.

Separate a dimensional characteristic from a material property or assembly requirement. An O-ring cross-section study, a hardness result and an assembly leakage test answer different questions. Request the evidence relevant to each requirement that matters for your application.

For a multi-cavity tool, discuss how the study represents cavity differences. Keep material-lot changes, tool maintenance and relevant process changes identifiable in the record. Combining results without that context can make the source of variation difficult to investigate.

Ask how the index was estimated

Confirm that the analysis uses the correct drawing limits and explains the estimate of variation. Ask how process stability and the suitability of the statistical model were assessed. If predicted nonconforming quantities are reported, keep the assumptions with the prediction.

State the capability requirement agreed for the project and who accepts the study. The same numerical target does not automatically describe every feature, material test or customer program. A change in drawing limits or production conditions may also change the basis of the comparison.

Connect monitoring to an action plan

Discuss the response to a process signal or a measured nonconformance. The plan should identify who investigates, how affected material is identified and what evidence is needed before normal supply resumes. Process monitoring and inspection each have a role in the agreed control plan.

Use lot-linked records to distinguish an observed trend from a proposed explanation. A suspected material, tool or molding cause needs supporting investigation. Record the action and how its effectiveness will be evaluated.
