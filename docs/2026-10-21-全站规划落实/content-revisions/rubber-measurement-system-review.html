Evaluate a rubber-part measurement system for the characteristic and decision it must support. Identify the method, instrument, specimen state, operators and study design before interpreting a Gage R&R number. Calibration records and a measurement-system study provide different information; review both within their scope.

The question is whether variation in the measurement process is understood well enough for the intended inspection or process decision. A display resolution or an instrument model alone does not answer it.

Define the measurement task

State the characteristic, units, drawing tolerance or material requirement, sample preparation and measurement method. For a dimensional check, identify the datum, support and contact arrangement. For hardness, identify the scale, specimen and method conditions rather than describing the task simply as “durometer.”

Use the part and fixture actually relevant to the decision. A study for a simple reference artifact does not automatically describe a different rubber geometry, surface or setup.

Distinguish the sources of variation

NIST's gauge-study guidance considers repeatability, reproducibility, stability and bias, with the artifacts, operators, instruments and configurations included in the study.

Agree how those terms are defined in your study. Repeated readings under the same defined conditions address one source of variation; changing operators, setups or time can introduce other sources. The study design determines which effects can be separated and reported.

Parts and property rangeWhat population and characteristic values do the selected samples represent?

Keep the denominator with a percentage

If a report expresses measurement variation as a percentage, identify the calculation basis. A percentage of a tolerance range and a percentage of a study's observed variation are not interchangeable descriptions. Keep the units, analysis method and customer acceptance criterion visible.

Do not apply one numerical threshold to every measurement task without its specified basis. Review how the result affects the actual decision: accepting a dimension, comparing materials or interpreting a process signal.

Use the findings to improve the measurement process

If operators or setups produce different results, investigate the corresponding conditions before assigning the variation to production. Review instructions, sample support, equipment condition and the measurement method. Document the proposed change and the evidence needed to evaluate it.

Keep calibration scope and validity with the instrument record. Then assess the measurement as used on the relevant samples. A valid calibration record does not eliminate every effect of handling, geometry or setup.

Connect MSA with the quality decision

Use the agreed measurement basis when reading SPC and process-capability evidence. If a PPAP submission requires MSA information, confirm the customer's method and acceptance requirements before preparing the study.
