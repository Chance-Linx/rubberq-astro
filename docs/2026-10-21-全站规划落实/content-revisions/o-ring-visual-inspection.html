Agree the O-ring surface acceptance requirements before approving samples or production. State the applicable standard edition and quality grade, drawing requirements and inspection arrangement. A photograph can identify a concern, but acceptance needs the relevant criteria and a clear record of the part being assessed.

ISO 3601-3 addresses quality acceptance criteria and surface imperfections for O-rings. Use the applicable document to assess a specific imperfection; the standard's public summary does not provide the complete limits.

Put the acceptance basis on the purchasing specification

Give the supplier the O-ring dimensions, material specification, drawing revision and intended service. Include the standard edition and amendments required by the project, the selected quality grade and any additional customer requirements. Resolve differences between the purchase order, drawing and inspection instruction before production approval.

Keep dimensional tolerances, material properties and surface acceptance identifiable in the specification. A hardness value or material-family name cannot describe the permitted surface condition. Likewise, a dimensional inspection result does not record what was found on the surface.

Record what you see before deciding what it means

Identify the part, lot and quantity examined. Photograph the feature with a scale and enough context to show its location around the ring and across its cross-section. Record the viewing conditions and measurement method so the supplier can repeat the observation.

Observation to recordInformation useful for supplier review
Raised material or a visible mould lineLocation, measured size and whether the feature extends around the ring
Depression, opening or missing materialShape, dimensions, distribution and photographs from more than one angle
Mark, cut or local damageLocation, handling history and whether the part has been installed
Discolouration or surface depositPackaging, storage conditions, contact history and a retained sample

These descriptions help communicate the observation. Classifying the feature and deciding acceptance still require the applicable specification. Avoid assigning a pass or fail from a general defect photograph without the necessary dimensions and criteria.

Separate incoming condition from installation damage

State whether the O-ring was examined as received, after cleaning, after assembly or after service. Keep unused parts from the same lot available for comparison. Record any tools, lubricants or cleaning agents used and retain the packaging identification.

If leakage or damage appeared during operation, include the groove drawing, assembly sequence, media, pressure and temperature history. Surface appearance is one part of that investigation. Agree the inspection and reporting arrangement

Before ordering, discuss which characteristics will be checked, how samples will be selected, what equipment and viewing conditions will be used, and what report is required. Specify the handling of suspect parts and who can approve a deviation. Keep these arrangements connected to the drawing and purchase order.

When a concern arises, report the number examined and the number affected, together with lot identification and representative images. This gives the supplier a defined population to investigate and helps distinguish an isolated observation from a recurring issue.

Prepare a clear O-ring enquiry

Send the drawing, material requirement, standard references, quantity and intended application. If the enquiry concerns an existing part, add labelled photographs and the incoming or service history.
