A rubber life estimate needs a defined property, a failure threshold and exposure data that support the proposed model. An elevated-temperature test result alone does not establish years of service for a finished component. Review what reached the threshold, under which conditions, before using an estimate in a supplier or design decision.

Start with the function that matters. A loss of tensile elongation, a change in hardness and a force-retention requirement describe different endpoints. Decide which endpoint is relevant before comparing estimated lives.

Separate an aging result from an extrapolation

ASTM D573 addresses deterioration of rubber properties under specified air-oven exposure. A reported change after an exposure is a test result. Estimating behaviour at another temperature or over a longer time adds a model and its assumptions.

ISO 11346 addresses thermal-endurance estimation using Arrhenius and WLF approaches. Its scope bases estimates on changes in selected properties after elevated-temperature exposure. The choice of property remains part of the meaning of the estimate.

Read the plot with the endpoint definition

An Arrhenius life plot commonly relates a logarithmic measure of time to reciprocal absolute temperature for a defined endpoint. Read both axes, their units and the fitted relation. Distinguish measured endpoint times from values extrapolated beyond the measured range.

The Arrhenius approach concerns chemical degradation; physical relaxation and chemical changes require careful separation in selecting a model. A straight fitted line does not, by itself, establish that the same mechanism governs all temperatures or every service condition.

Check whether the application changes the question

Document the component's contacting media, temperature cycle, mechanical load and installation. Identify cleaning, cold starts, pressure events or other conditions that are absent from the laboratory exposure. Ask which additional component evaluation is needed to address those differences.

Do not transfer an estimate for an unloaded specimen directly to a loaded seal or bonded mount. Keep material aging, geometry, force retention and assembly function as distinct evidence. The compression-set and stress-relaxation comparison helps define the relevant compression measurement.

Use the estimate to plan validation

State the decision the estimate will support: choosing candidates, setting a trial exposure or evaluating a proposed service requirement. Request the underlying observations and assumptions needed for that decision. Avoid converting a screening comparison into a universal component-life promise.

For recurring supply, retain the approved compound identification, drawing revision and evaluation basis. Agree how a material or process change will affect the evidence required for continued approval.
