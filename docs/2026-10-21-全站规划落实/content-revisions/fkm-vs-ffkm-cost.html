Evaluate an FKM-to-FFKM change by comparing the actual seal specification, operating conditions, validation effort and cost of a seal-related interruption. Request quotations for the same geometry and quantity, using identified candidate compounds. A fixed price multiple does not describe every material, part or order.

The useful purchasing question is which candidate can meet the documented requirements and what it takes to approve and maintain that choice. An upgrade should resolve a defined problem with an agreed test plan.

Describe the application before comparing quotations

Provide the drawing revision, inside diameter and cross-section or other critical dimensions, mating geometry and installation method. Identify the complete process exposure, including media, concentration, cleaning and temperature cycles. For equipment service, include the seal location and operating history.

When an existing seal has failed, retain photographs, unused reference parts and the conditions recorded around the failure. These inputs help distinguish a material question from damage during installation, a geometry issue or a change in operating conditions.

Keep material family and compound specification separate

FKM and FFKM are material families. The candidate for approval needs an identifiable formulation or grade, relevant data and a defined inspection basis. Ask which test conditions apply to the information being compared.

Review hardness, physical properties, exposure and functional checks as a connected requirement. Where purity, outgassing or plasma exposure matters, identify the relevant method and acceptance criteria for the particular process. A family name does not establish those results for an individual compound.

Separate one-time development or tooling charges from recurring part prices. Ask for the assumptions behind lead-time and supply discussions so procurement and engineering evaluate the same scope.

Compare the consequences of the decision

Use your own maintenance and operating records to estimate what a seal change could affect. Relevant items may include part consumption, planned replacement work, investigation time, downtime and the effort to qualify an alternative. Keep estimates separate from observed results.

A lower purchase price can be assessed alongside the work needed to approve and maintain the material. A higher purchase price also needs a clear technical reason. Compare both against your actual process, rather than assigning a universal cost ratio or service-life advantage to a polymer family.

Agree the validation before changing supply

Define what a sample must demonstrate and who accepts the result. Include fit and assembly checks with the material evaluation. Equipment-specific requirements may need a trial under representative operating conditions before the change is approved.

For an existing branded part, provide the reference together with the original specification and available dimensions. The reference helps identify the part being discussed; approval of a replacement depends on the application review and validation.
