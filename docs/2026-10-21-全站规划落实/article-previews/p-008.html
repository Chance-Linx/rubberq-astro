Low-Temperature Retraction: Use TR Data with Seal Requirements

A low-temperature retraction result describes a specimen's recovery under a specified test procedure. Use it with the actual compound and service requirements; it is not an automatic minimum operating temperature for every seal made from the same polymer family.

For a cold-start problem, distinguish the material comparison from the assembled component's task. A static enclosure seal, a moving seal and a part that must recover after opening can need different functional evaluations even when they use related materials.

Identify what the TR report actually measures

ASTM D1329 describes a temperature-retraction procedure for evaluating crystallization effects and comparing low-temperature viscoelastic behaviour. Its public scope positions the method alongside other low-temperature tests for selecting materials.

Reports can identify temperatures at specified retraction points, such as TR10 or TR70. Confirm how each point is defined in the applicable method and retain the specimen preparation, imposed extension and temperature procedure with the result. Do not compare different points as if they were the same property.

Keep different low-temperature properties distinct

EvidenceQuestion it helps address
Temperature retractionHow the specified specimen recovers through the method's temperature sequence
Other material low-temperature testsThe particular response defined by that method and specimen
Cold assembly or operating testWhether the actual component meets its functional requirement under the specified conditions
Warm-up and recovery observationsHow the component behaves through the application's temperature transition

A named material-temperature range should not replace these definitions. Identify whether a supplier figure is a test result, a data-sheet description or an application recommendation, and request the basis relevant to your decision.

Compare candidates under a common basis

Identify the compound code, lot, cure and conditioning state. Use the same applicable method and report the test conditions before ranking results. A value for one FKM grade does not establish the result for all FKMs or a substitute compound.

Where storage or sustained cold exposure matters, describe the duration and recovery requirement explicitly. If a report does not represent that history, agree the additional evidence needed rather than assuming a temperature point covers every exposure duration.

Define what must happen at cold start

For the component trial, state the minimum and normal temperatures, time at temperature, pressure or load, movement and any required opening or closing sequence. Identify the contacting media and installation conditions. Agree when leakage, force, movement or another functional response will be checked.

Record the observations during cooling, cold operation and warm-up as required by the application. Keep the fixture, mating dimensions and material identification visible so a material change can be compared without losing the assembly context.

Bring failed-part evidence into the review

If the request follows a cold-service failure, retain the part and document when the condition appeared. Record cracking, leakage or delayed recovery as observations before assigning a cause. Include installation, storage and temperature history with photographs and lot identification.

Send RubberQ the cold-service requirements, drawing and existing material reports. Review the seal specification and agree the material and component evaluation needed for the actual duty.
