Post-Curing Silicone Parts: Specify the Material and Evaluation

Specify post-curing as a defined treatment linked to a particular silicone compound and the results your component must achieve. “Post-cured” alone does not identify the temperature, duration, loading arrangement or acceptance evidence. The need for the step and the appropriate cycle depend on the material and application.

Keep the original material and service requirements visible. A treatment decision should address the properties and contamination concerns that matter to the component, rather than adding an oven step without a measurable purpose.

Start with the compound and cure system

WACKER's solid and liquid silicone processing guide discusses post-curing in relation to mechanical properties, volatile components and the cure system. Its distinction between material systems is important: use guidance for the actual compound, not a cycle copied from a different grade.

Identify the material designation, cure system and supplier instructions with the part drawing. If a proposed compound is intended to meet its technical requirements without post-curing, evaluate that claim against the actual finished-part requirements. Do not assume all silicones require the same treatment or that omitting it is always acceptable.

Define which outcome the treatment must support

RequirementEvidence to agree
Dimensional conditionDrawing revision, specimen state and the timing and method of measurement
Hardness or mechanical propertiesApplicable method, sample preparation and acceptance limits
Compression setCompression, exposure, recovery and measurement conditions
Force retentionA separately defined force-decay evaluation where required
Volatile or contamination concernThe relevant output, treatment state, test method and equipment acceptance basis

Compression set and retained force answer different questions. Use the comparison of compression set and stress relaxation to keep their test requirements separate. Neither a material property nor a treatment record supplies the complete result of an installed assembly test.

Describe the treatment as a controlled operation

For a proposed cycle, identify the set conditions and how the treatment time is defined. Discuss loading, part separation, air circulation where applicable, lot identification and the relevant equipment records. The material instructions and actual geometry should guide the process review.

Agree how samples will represent the production arrangement. A small specimen treated separately from the proposed production load should not silently become proof for that load. Keep any trial changes and the resulting measurements connected to the same material and part revision.

Compare before and after on a defined basis

If the purpose is to evaluate a treatment's effect, select matched samples and record the conditions for both groups. State the property, measurement method and acceptance decision before reviewing the result. Do not infer improvement in every property because one measurement changed.

For volatile measurements, identify the reported quantity and sample preparation. A weight change under one exposure, a vacuum outgassing result and an assembly contamination result are different evidence. Choose the one that addresses the application's requirement and retain the test conditions.

Carry the approved state into repeat supply

Once the material and sample route are agreed, specify the required finished-part state and records in the order documentation. Discuss how treatment or material changes will be reviewed and which checks are needed after a change.

Send the silicone-part drawing and property requirements, existing material instructions and intended service conditions.
